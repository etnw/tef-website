---
title: "Commercial due diligence"
anchor: "due-diligence"
navLabel: "Commercial due diligence"
order: 11
lead: >-
  Independent assessment of whether customers will buy the target's product at the price, in the volume, and on the timeline the investment case assumes.
blocks:
  - label: "Delivered"
    body: >-
      market need, tested in conversations with target customers. TAM and SAM from applications, unit volumes, and price points, set against the company's own figures. Competitive position against incumbents and other startups, including competitors' market share, roadmaps, and pricing. Path to revenue: design-in and qualification requirements, realistic time to production volume, and dependencies on foundry, OSAT, and channel partners. Customer base and pipeline, separating paying customers from pilots and evaluations. Pricing, margin, and sales model. Whether the team can close production contracts, and whether planned hires follow revenue rather than the funding round. A short written report for the investment committee or board: what the evidence supports, assumptions still to be tested, risks ranked by impact, and questions for the founders.
  - label: "Not included"
    body: >-
      technical, legal, and financial diligence. These stay with the investor's specialists, and we work alongside them.
  - label: "Applies when"
    body: >-
      evaluating a venture investment, a minority stake, or an acquisition, or preparing for commercial integration once a transaction has been agreed.
  - label: "Sequence"
    body: >-
      after the transaction, the partner who did the diligence can lead the commercial work: a 90-day plan that closes the gaps the diligence found, embedded commercial leadership, and the first sales hire. Nothing gets lost between the deal and the plan.
---
